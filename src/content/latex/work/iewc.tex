\publicationabstract{Elastic Weight Consolidation (EWC) controls forgetting with a quadratic penalty, yet its empirical Fisher is usually justified through probabilistic curvature. We formulate \emph{Continual Learning as an Optimization Constraint} (CLOC), preserving chosen functionals of previously learned behavior. First-order preservation of any differentiable scalar functional yields a gradient outer product; for per-sample negative log likelihood, it is exactly EWC's empirical Fisher, without a model-Fisher interpretation. Our central method, Improved Elastic Weight Consolidation (IEWC), instead preserves outputs modulo motions tangent to old loss level sets, separating EWC's implicit loss-gradient weighting from parameter sensitivity. IEWC is specified by $(\lambda,\kappa,\Gamma,h,\Pi)$: preservation strength, relative damping, output geometry, sample weights, and matrix approximation. A contamination experiment confirms that normalization prevents corrupt samples from dominating the full matrix. Across five domains, IEWC variants improve EWC's selected means in classification, segmentation, and language, while remaining comparable in forecasting and diffusion. In a validation-selected locked study, uniform IEWC improves ImageNet-R final average accuracy over matched EWC by 2.00 percentage points (95\% CI 0.93--3.07) and gives no material mean difference on TRACE ($-0.11$; 95\% CI $-1.48$--$1.27$).}

\section{From preservation constraints to IEWC}

Continual Learning as an Optimization Constraint (CLOC) starts from a direct requirement: preserve chosen functionals of previously learned behavior while learning a new task. First-order preservation of any differentiable scalar functional yields a gradient outer product; for per-sample negative log likelihood this is exactly EWC's empirical Fisher, without a model-Fisher interpretation. IEWC instead preserves outputs modulo motions tangent to the old loss level sets, which separates EWC's implicit loss-gradient weighting from parameter sensitivity. It is specified by preservation strength, relative damping, output geometry, sample weights, and matrix approximation.

\section{Main results}

A contamination experiment confirms that normalization prevents corrupt samples from dominating the full importance matrix. Across five domains, IEWC variants improve EWC's selected means in classification, segmentation, and language, while remaining comparable in forecasting and diffusion. In a validation-selected locked study, uniform IEWC improves ImageNet-R final average accuracy over matched EWC by 2.00 percentage points (95\% CI 0.93--3.07) and gives no material mean difference on TRACE ($-0.11$; 95\% CI $-1.48$--$1.27$).
