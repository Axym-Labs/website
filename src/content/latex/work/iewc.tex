\begin{publicationsummary}
\publicationsummarypart{Problem}{Elastic Weight Consolidation (EWC) is a widely used regularization-based technique for continual learning, protecting performance on old tasks with a quadratic penalty whose empirical Fisher is usually justified through probabilistic curvature.}
\publicationsummarypart{Idea}{We instead introduce \emph{Continual Learning as an Optimization Constraint} (CLOC): new training must preserve chosen functionals of learned behavior. EWC's empirical Fisher is exactly the first-order form of this constraint when the preserved functional is each sample's loss.}
\publicationsummarypart{Insight}{This derivation exposes a flaw: EWC implicitly weights each sample by the scale of its loss gradient, so well-fitted samples lose importance. EWC-DR observed this failure in classification; CLOC identifies its general cause.}
\publicationsummarypart{Method}{Improved Elastic Weight Consolidation (IEWC) penalizes output changes according to the first-order approximation of each old sample's loss level set, removing the weighting for any differentiable loss and output geometry, and prevents corrupt samples from dominating the importance matrix.}
\publicationsummarypart{Evidence}{Across classification, segmentation, language, forecasting, and diffusion, IEWC variants match or improve on EWC and outperform EWC-DR. In a held-out evaluation with validation-selected hyperparameters, uniform IEWC improves ImageNet-R accuracy over matched EWC by 2.00 percentage points (95\% CI 0.93--3.07).}
\publicationsummarypart{Significance}{More broadly, deriving a regularizer from what a model should preserve, rather than from an approximation of curvature, gives a principled recipe for designing continual-learning penalties.}
\end{publicationsummary}
