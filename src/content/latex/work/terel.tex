\begin{publicationsummary}
\publicationsummarypart{Problem}{Layer-local self-supervised objectives remove the backward pass between layers, but almost all still compute their loss over a batch of samples and are therefore local in space but not in time.}
\publicationsummarypart{Idea}{We introduce Temporal Regularized Learning (TeReL), a spatially and temporally local learning rule for self-supervised representation learning from sequential input streams. The key idea is to instantiate a VICReg-style objective with temporally adjacent positives and non-differentiated running population statistics, yielding a per-neuron online loss with bounded state.}
\publicationsummarypart{Theory}{The objective is a soft form of Slow Feature Analysis, and its gradient is a Hebbian update of the feedforward weights and an anti-Hebbian update of one lateral matrix per layer.}
\publicationsummarypart{Evidence}{The experiments show that the rule works in a small network. On a class-chunked MNIST stream, a two-layer fully connected network trained with batch size one outperforms random features (96.48\% versus 95.09\% linear-probe accuracy) and classical SFA, and a variant that relaxes both locality constraints is comparable to supervised backpropagation.}
\publicationsummarypart{Significance}{More broadly, TeReL shows that a modern self-supervised objective can be learned one sample at a time with purely local updates, bringing representation learning closer to what brains and neuromorphic hardware can implement.}
\end{publicationsummary}
