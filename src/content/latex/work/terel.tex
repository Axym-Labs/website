\publicationabstract{VICReg organizes noncontrastive representation learning around invariance, marginal variance, and cross-coordinate covariance. Its usual batch statistics and two-view computation do not directly yield a causal, neuron-local update. Temporal Regularized Learning (TeReL) converts this decomposition into a causal surrogate: adjacent observations form the positive pair, detached running moments replace batch statistics, and a lagged same-layer operator supplies a covariance signal. Temporal adjacency turns invariance into slowness, making the construction a soft form of Slow Feature Analysis. Detaching historical quantities truncates cross-time credit assignment without removing the current-observation gradient. At each layer, a detached regularized target maps its residual to the state of each neuron. The feedforward update then factors into a presynaptic value and this postsynaptic state; one lateral matrix corrects the state once and receives an anti-Hebbian state--state contribution. The resulting encoder processes one observation at a time, sends no error signal across learned layers or time, and stores three causal scalars per neuron together with one matrix of auxiliary lateral parameters per layer. On a class-chunked MNIST stream, batch-size-one stochastic-gradient training reaches $96.39\pm0.19\%$ held-out linear-probe accuracy.}

\section{A causal VICReg construction}

\href{https://arxiv.org/abs/2105.04906}{VICReg} separates representation learning into invariance between paired views, marginal variance, and cross-coordinate decorrelation. TeReL preserves these three roles while replacing the second view by the preceding observation, batch moments by detached running moments, and the differentiated batch-covariance signal by a lagged same-layer operator. Because the positive pair consists of adjacent observations, its invariance term is a soft \href{https://doi.org/10.1162/089976602317318938}{Slow Feature Analysis} objective.

For current activation $z_t$, preceding same-sequence activation $p_t$, detached running mean $\mu_t$ and variance $v_t$, and lateral matrix $M_t$, the surrogate gradient is proportional to

\[
r_t
=I_t(z_t-p_t)
-\frac{\lambda_V}{\lambda_T}[\tau-v_t]_+\odot(z_t-\mu_t)
+\frac{\lambda_C}{2\lambda_T}\operatorname{offdiag}(M_t)(z_t-\mu_t).
\]

The three terms supply temporal agreement, variance expansion below the target $\tau$, and covariance control. For the detached target $\widehat z_t=\operatorname{sg}(z_t-r_t)$, the local loss $\lambda_T\lVert z_t-\widehat z_t\rVert_2^2/D$ has the same derivative with respect to $z_t$ as the weighted surrogate at its construction point. This is a causal surrogate of VICReg's decomposition, not a scalar re-expression of the original batch objective: its gradient depends on the current observation and fixed-size detached quantities, but not on the history that produced them.

\section{From target residual to neuron-local update}

Mapping the residual through the activation derivative gives $b_t=J_{\phi,t}^{\top}r_t$. One same-layer correction defines the signed postsynaptic state

\[
s_t=b_t-\eta\kappa M_tb_t.
\]

The feedforward gradient then factors as

\[
\nabla_{W_t}\widetilde\ell_a
=\frac{2\lambda_T}{D}s_tx_t^{\top}.
\]

Each feedforward synapse therefore reads a presynaptic scalar and a postsynaptic scalar. The same lateral matrix supplies the covariance signal and state correction, while its state--state contribution is anti-Hebbian under the inhibitory sign convention. The output is detached before entering the next learned layer. A width-$D$ layer retains $3D$ causal scalars, one boundary-validity bit, and $D^2$ auxiliary lateral parameters; the reported online rule uses plain stochastic gradient descent without optimizer moments.

\section{Results}

On the class-chunked MNIST stream, TeReL reaches $96.39\pm0.19\%$ held-out linear-probe accuracy. Random features reach $95.14\pm0.27\%$, batch SFA reaches $89.52\pm0.13\%$, and incremental SFA reaches $88.70\pm0.09\%$. Layer-local supervised contrastive learning reaches $96.95\pm0.13\%$ while using labels, minibatches, batch normalization, and AdamW. The spatially and temporally relaxed TeReL-Offline reference reaches $98.39\pm0.04\%$, compared with $98.17\pm0.07\%$ for fully supervised offline learning.

Randomly shuffling the stream reduces TeReL accuracy by $1.31$ paired percentage points, and removing the temporal term reduces it by $1.84$. Removing variance control yields a nearly constant representation at $15.10\pm0.18\%$ accuracy; removing covariance control makes all five evaluations non-finite. With a validation-selected continuation step, unlabeled online updates retain $96.43\pm0.15\%$ accuracy, compared with $96.39\pm0.19\%$ for the fixed encoder.
