\publicationabstract{Layer-local self-supervised objectives remove the backward pass between layers, but almost all still compute their loss over a batch of samples and are therefore local in space but not in time. We introduce Temporal Regularized Learning (TeReL), a spatially and temporally local learning rule for self-supervised representation learning from sequential input streams. The key idea is to instantiate a VICReg-style objective with temporally adjacent positives and non-differentiated running population statistics, yielding a per-neuron online loss with bounded state. The objective is a soft form of Slow Feature Analysis, and its gradient is a Hebbian update of the feedforward weights and an anti-Hebbian update of one lateral matrix per layer. The aim of the experiments is to show that the rule works in a small network, not to compete with backpropagation. On a class-chunked MNIST stream, a two-layer fully connected network trained with batch size one outperforms random features (96.48\% versus 95.09\% linear-probe accuracy) and classical SFA, and a variant that relaxes both locality constraints is comparable to supervised backpropagation. Because training and inference use the same computation, the network can keep learning from unlabeled data during inference without loss of accuracy.}

\section{A causal VICReg construction}

\href{https://arxiv.org/abs/2105.04906}{VICReg} separates representation learning into invariance between paired views, marginal variance, and cross-coordinate decorrelation. TeReL preserves these three roles while replacing the second view by the preceding observation, batch moments by detached running moments, and the differentiated batch-covariance signal by a lagged same-layer operator. Because the positive pair consists of adjacent observations, its invariance term is a soft \href{https://doi.org/10.1162/089976602317318938}{Slow Feature Analysis} objective.

For current activation $z_t$, preceding activation $p_t$, detached running mean $\mu_t$ and variance $v_t$, and lateral matrix $M_t$, the surrogate gradient is proportional to

\[
r_t
=I_t(z_t-p_t)
-\frac{\lambda_V}{\lambda_T}[\tau-v_t]_+\odot(z_t-\mu_t)
+\frac{\lambda_C}{2\lambda_T}\operatorname{offdiag}(M_t)(z_t-\mu_t).
\]

The three terms control temporal slowness, variance expansion below the target $\tau$, and covariance. For the detached target $\widehat z_t=\operatorname{sg}(z_t-r_t)$, the local loss $\lambda_T\lVert z_t-\widehat z_t\rVert_2^2/D$ has the same derivative with respect to $z_t$ as the weighted surrogate at its construction point. This is a causal surrogate of VICReg's decomposition, not a scalar re-expression of the original batch objective: its gradient depends on the current observation and fixed-size detached quantities, but not on the history that produced them.

\section{From target residual to neuron-local update}

Mapping the residual through the activation derivative gives $b_t=J_{\phi,t}^{\top}r_t$. One same-layer correction defines the signed postsynaptic state

\[
s_t=b_t-\eta\kappa M_tb_t.
\]

The feedforward gradient then factors as

\[
\nabla_{W_t}\widetilde\ell_a
=\frac{2\lambda_T}{D}s_tx_t^{\top}.
\]

Each feedforward synapse therefore reads a presynaptic scalar and a postsynaptic scalar. The same lateral matrix supplies the covariance signal and state correction, while its state--state contribution is anti-Hebbian under the inhibitory sign convention. The output is detached before entering the next learned layer. A width-$D$ layer retains $3D$ causal scalars, one boundary-validity bit, and $D^2$ auxiliary lateral parameters; the reported online rule uses plain stochastic gradient descent without optimizer moments.

\section{Results}

On a class-chunked MNIST stream, TeReL reaches $96.48\pm0.15\%$ held-out linear-probe accuracy, a paired gain of 1.39 percentage points over random features ($95.09\pm0.23\%$). Batch SFA reaches $90.07\pm0.26\%$ and incremental SFA $89.98\pm0.09\%$. Layer-local supervised contrastive learning reaches $96.95\pm0.17\%$ while using labels and minibatches at every layer. The spatially and temporally relaxed TeReL-Offline reference reaches $98.41\pm0.04\%$, on par with fully supervised offline learning at $98.44\pm0.09\%$.

Shuffling the stream reduces TeReL accuracy by $1.08$ paired percentage points, and removing the temporal term reduces it by $1.50$. Removing the variance term collapses the representation to $15.36\pm0.27\%$ accuracy; removing the covariance term makes training diverge for all five initializations. With a validation-selected step size, unlabeled online updates during evaluation reach $96.50\pm0.15\%$, compared with $96.48\pm0.15\%$ for the fixed encoder.
